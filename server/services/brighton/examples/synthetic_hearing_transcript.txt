Ninth Judicial District Court, Juniper County, State of Utah
Whitlock v. Whitlock, Case No. 990000123
Hearing on Petitioner's Motion to Enforce Parent-Time and Child Support
March 12, 2031 — Commissioner Teodoro Vance presiding (assigned judge: Judge Imani Okonkwo)

[Commissioner Vance]: We're on the record in Whitlock versus Whitlock, case number 990000123. Today is March 12, 2031. This is the time set for Petitioner's motion to enforce parent-time and child support. Appearances, please.

[Ms. Arroyo]: Good morning, Commissioner. Lucinda Arroyo of Arroyo Family Law for the Petitioner, Dana Whitlock, who is present in person with me at counsel table.

[Mr. Pell]: Good morning. Gideon Pell of Pell and Marsh for the Respondent, Corwin Whitlock. Mr. Whitlock is appearing by WebEx, and I'm here in the courtroom.

[Commissioner Vance]: Thank you. I've read the motion, the response, and the reply. Ms. Arroyo, it's your motion. Keep it brief, I've read everything.

[Ms. Arroyo]: Thank you. Three things, Commissioner. First, the weekend exchanges. The decree sets Friday exchanges at five o'clock at the Juniper Public Library. Since October, Mr. Whitlock has been late to eleven of fourteen exchanges, sometimes by more than an hour. My client and the two children sit in the parking lot waiting. Second, child support. He's behind. Third, he changed jobs in the fall and has never filed an updated financial declaration, so nobody can tell whether support is even calculated correctly.

[Commissioner Vance]: Mr. Pell?

[Mr. Pell]: Commissioner, my client doesn't dispute that he's been late. He took a new position at the distribution center in November and his shift ends at five thirty. He physically cannot be at the library at five. He's asked to move the time and hasn't gotten an answer. On support, we agree there are arrears. We've conferred with counsel and we can stipulate to the number.

[Commissioner Vance]: What's the number?

[Mr. Pell]: One thousand eight hundred fifty dollars through the end of February 2031.

[Ms. Arroyo]: That's correct, Commissioner. We stipulate to $1,850 through February.

[Commissioner Vance]: All right. And the financial declaration?

[Mr. Pell]: He hasn't filed one. He'll do it. He needs a little time to get the pay stubs together.

[Commissioner Vance]: He's had since November. Ms. Arroyo, anything on communication? I saw a lot of text messages attached to your reply.

[Ms. Arroyo]: Yes. The texting between the parties has gotten unproductive. We'd ask that communication go through a co-parenting application so there's a record. And we're asking for a finding of contempt on the late exchanges.

[Mr. Pell]: We don't object to an application. We do object to contempt. This was a scheduling problem, not willful disobedience.

[Commissioner Vance]: Mr. Whitlock, can you hear me all right on WebEx?

[Mr. Whitlock]: Yes, sir, I can hear you.

[Commissioner Vance]: Your shift ends at five thirty. How long is the drive to the library?

[Mr. Whitlock]: About twenty minutes, sir. I can be there by six without any trouble.

[Commissioner Vance]: Ms. Whitlock, does six o'clock work on your end?

[Ms. Whitlock]: Six is fine, Commissioner, as long as it's actually six.

[Commissioner Vance]: Understood. All right, here is my ruling, and I'm going to go through it item by item so counsel can get it into an order.

First, parent-time exchanges. The Friday exchange is moved from five o'clock to six o'clock p.m. Location stays the same, the Juniper Public Library parking lot. The Sunday return remains at six o'clock p.m. at the same location. There is a fifteen-minute grace period. If the receiving parent is not there by six fifteen and has not sent notice through the application, the waiting parent may leave, and that weekend's parent-time is forfeited. I'm not going to have children sitting in a parking lot for an hour.

Second, child support arrears. Based on the stipulation of the parties, I find arrears of $1,850 through February 28, 2031. Mr. Whitlock shall pay that amount in full within thirty days of today, and the payment goes through the Office of Recovery Services, not directly to Ms. Whitlock. Ongoing support stays as it is in the decree for now.

Third, the financial declaration. Mr. Whitlock shall file and serve an updated financial declaration, with his two most recent pay stubs attached, within fourteen days of today. If the numbers show support needs to be recalculated, either party can file the appropriate motion.

Fourth, communication. Both parties shall enroll in a co-parenting communication application within seven days. Counsel can agree on which one. Once you're both enrolled, all non-emergency communication about the children goes through the application. Emergencies, pick up the phone.

Fifth, contempt. I'm reserving it. I'm not finding contempt today. Mr. Whitlock, the schedule problem was real, but you also did not come to the Court to fix it, you just showed up late for four months. The issue of contempt and the request for attorney fees are both reserved, and I'll look at how the next two months go.

Which brings me to the last thing. I'm setting a review hearing for May 14, 2031, at nine o'clock a.m., in this courtroom, in front of me. If the exchanges are on time, the arrears are paid, and the declaration is filed, that will be a short hearing.

Ms. Arroyo, you'll prepare the order.

[Ms. Arroyo]: Yes, Commissioner. Thank you.

[Mr. Pell]: Thank you, Commissioner. Just to confirm, the thirty days on the arrears runs from today?

[Commissioner Vance]: From today. That makes it April 11, 2031. And the declaration is due March 26, 2031. Anything further?

[Ms. Arroyo]: Nothing from Petitioner.

[Mr. Pell]: Nothing further.

[Commissioner Vance]: We're adjourned. Thank you, everyone.
